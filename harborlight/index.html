Executive summary

On the morning of 4 February 2026 Harborlight Dental & Insurance found ransom notes in every shared folder. Coastal MSP, the five-person IT contractor, had no SOC and no alert queue, but full Sysmon and Windows Security logging existed and had never been reviewed. I reconstructed the intrusion from that telemetry, from initial access to impact.

What happened. Emily Grant, a front-office user, received a spoofed vendor-invoice email. Her workstation (BACKOFFICE-PC1) downloaded a .7z archive containing an ISO, which bypassed Mark-of-the-Web, and ran rundll32 review.dll,StartW from the mounted drive at about 02:25 UTC (a first attempt ran at 01:59). The implant injected into notepad.exe, hijacked the ms-settings registry handler to abuse fodhelper.exe for a UAC bypass, and launched a fake C:\Users\Public\taskhostw.exe at 02:42. That binary injected into spoolsv.exe, which read LSASS at 02:49. The stolen Emily.Grant credential was then used over SMB admin shares to reach the file server HL-FS01 (03:34) and the domain controller ADDC01 (04:28).

Impact. On HL-FS01 the attacker renamed rclone to MsMpEng.exe and copied C:\Shares to a Mega account between 03:58 and 04:04. Shadow copies, boot recovery and the backup catalog were destroyed twice (04:04 and 04:05). The attacker dumped the Active Directory database with ntdsutil (04:33), planted three command-and-control implants (fake taskhostw.exe, OneDriveSync.exe, wsync.exe) beaconing to cdn.cloud-endpoint.net on Cloudflare IPs, wiped event logs in two waves (05:13–05:20), triggered the Akira encryption alert (05:14), and created a backdoor domain account svc_sql that was added to Administrators (Event 4732, 05:22:28) and Domain Admins (Event 4728, 05:22:57). The encryptor dropped akira_readme.txt 128 times and produced .akira files.

Dwell time. C2 callbacks run from 02:25:08 to 18:47:29 UTC, at least 16 h 22 min of attacker access, about 13 h after the encryption. The telemetry never shows containment.

Why it was missed. The MSP dismissed the phishing alert (02:41) and the rundll32 alert as false positives, acknowledged but did not escalate the LSASS alert, and nobody reviewed the 03:45 cloud-storage exfiltration alert. A flat /24 network let one stolen credential move workstation to file server to domain controller unchecked.

Q1.1It has to run

Answer: (a) Execution is the gate.

Evidence

Q5.4What the access mask means

Answer: 0x1fffff = PROCESS_ALL_ACCESS.

Evidence
  • The mask on the LSASS and notepad.exe rows.

A request for every right on a process is far more than a monitoring tool needs. It lets the caller read and write the process's memory, which is what LSASS dumping requires.

Method and AI-use disclosure

I worked in Microsoft Sentinel against one unified table, HarborlightDental_CL, filtering on EventTime only and pivoting by EventID and host. The judgment rules I used throughout: read the full path, not the filename; ask whether the source and target pair makes sense; check the access mask; check the timing against the previous attacker step; and baseline against the hours before the attack. Where a query returned nothing, I changed fields rather than concluding there was nothing there (for example, Image versus CommandLine for MsMpEng.exe).

AI assistance. The hunt allowed AI for query formulation and technique research only. Claude (Anthropic) helped me write and fix KQL queries, triage exported CSVs, and word my reasoning. I read the telemetry and made the noise-versus-attack calls myself. Two examples: for Q2.2 I fed the noisy process-access results (a CSV) to Claude to narrow the search, then confirmed the notepad.exe rows in Sentinel and looked up why attackers use Notepad; and I corrected several of Claude's suggestions where the data disagreed (the Q11.3 dropper guess and the Q12.2 port doubt).
